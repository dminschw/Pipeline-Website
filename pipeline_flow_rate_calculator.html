The governing flow rate is whichever constraint is lower — a pipe can only carry as much gas as its tighter limit allows, whether that's the velocity you're willing to tolerate (erosion, noise, control-valve trim wear) or the pressure the pipeline segment can give up over its length under the Weymouth equation. Z is left as a simple editable number (default 1.0, ideal gas) — a rigorous design would pull Z from a correlation such as Standing-Katz or CNGA based on gas composition, pressure, and temperature. Wall thickness here is fixed to Standard weight (STD, equal to Schedule 40 for NPS 10" and smaller; a constant 0.375" for NPS 12" and larger per ASME B36.10) rather than a pressure-design calculation — for a full Barlow-equation pressure-design check, or to use a different schedule, use the diameter calculator instead. This is a preliminary/educational tool; verify results against the current edition of the applicable code before use in real design.
